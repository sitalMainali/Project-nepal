## Why the welcome email matters

- It arrives while the meal is still fresh in the guest's mind.
- It's the moment to turn a one-off booking into a relationship.

## When to send it

- Within 24 hours of the first visit or sign-up, not the moment they book.
- Mention the visit if your booking system tells you when it happened.

## What to include

1. A real thank-you, signed by a real person (owner, chef or manager).
2. One story: the producer behind a dish, the history of the building, the team.
3. One clear next step: book again, try a signature dish, or join a regular event.
4. What they'll get from you, and how often, so the next email is expected.

## What to leave out

- Discount codes on the first email (they teach guests to wait for a deal).
- Five buttons. One is enough.
- Stock photos. A quick photo of your pass beats a generic plate every time.

## A simple template

> Hi [first name], thanks for joining us on [day]. We hope the [dish] lived up to the hype. Next time, ask for [insider tip]. We'd love to see you again soon. [Name]

## Checklist before you hit send

- Consent: did the guest opt in to marketing? See [SMS and email consent basics](/resources/sms-consent-basics).
- Sender name is your venue, not "noreply".
- Unsubscribe link works.
